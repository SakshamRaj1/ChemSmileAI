Molecular Fingerprint ▶

Morgan fingerprints (circular fingerprints) are high-dimensional numerical vectors that map complex chemical structures into standardized bit arrays for computational molecular property analysis, virtual screening, and similarity mapping. Derived from the Morgan algorithm, they operate by assigning invariant identifiers to atoms in a molecular graph and iteratively updating them based on their surrounding radial chemical environments up to a user-defined bond radius.

2D Structure ▶

A 2D molecular structure is a flat representation of a molecule showing the arrangement of atoms and the bonds between them in two dimensions, without depicting the molecule's three-dimensional shape. It is not a hardcoded image, but a programmatic spatial mapping of a molecular graph onto a flat coordinate plane. This function renders chemical structures as SVG (Scalable Vector Graphics) ensures infinitely scalable, crisp drawings perfect for high-quality figures or web rendering.

3D Structure (Ball & Stick) ▶

A 3D molecular conformer is a specific, energetically stable three-dimensional arrangement of atoms in a molecule that can interconvert through the rotation of single bonds without breaking chemical bonds. a conformer captures the dynamic, physical shape of the molecule in 3D space. Because single bonds rotate freely, a single molecule can exist in thousands of different conformers—some of higher energy (less stable) and some of lower energy (more stable). This analysis is crucial for tasks such as clustering, diversity analysis, and scaffold-based splits. The Murcko scaffolds, a specific type of scaffold analysis, are used to remove all side chains and substituents, allowing for a chemically diverse set of molecules to be selected.
